The Absolute Basics

If you only read five definitions on this page, read these. Everything else on this site assumes you know what these five mean.

SAP
The world's most widely used enterprise software. When people in this industry say "SAP," they usually mean the system a company runs its finance, HR, and supply chain through — one system, instead of a dozen disconnected ones.
ERP Enterprise Resource Planning
The category of software SAP belongs to. One shared database running the parts of a business that used to live in separate spreadsheets and separate departments.
GRC Governance, Risk & Compliance
The discipline of making sure a company actually runs the way it says it runs — the right people have the right access, changes get approved before they go live, and the numbers can be trusted. Also the name of SAP's own GRC platform (see below).
ITGC IT General Controls
The baseline checks that make every other financial control trustworthy: who can access the system, how changes get made, whether the data is backed up. If ITGC is weak, an auditor can't trust anything built on top of it — which is most of what a company reports.
SOX Sarbanes-Oxley Act
The 2002 US law requiring public companies to prove, every year, that their financial reporting controls actually work. "SOX testing" is the industry's shorthand for that annual proof — and it's most of what this course trains you to do.
Controls & compliance

Controls & Compliance Terms

The vocabulary of the workpaper — what you're testing, what you found, and how bad it is.

Control
A safeguard designed to prevent or catch something going wrong. "Only the finance director can approve payments over $50,000" is a control.
Deficiency
What auditors call it when a control doesn't work the way it's supposed to. Every one of the 22 Books in this course is built around one.
Material Weakness
A deficiency serious enough that it could actually mislead someone reading the financial statements — the most severe rating a control failure can receive.
Significant Deficiency
A step down from material weakness — still serious enough to flag to the audit committee, not yet severe enough to undermine the financials on its own.
Segregation of Duties SoD
The rule that no one person should be able to both create and approve the same transaction — create a vendor and pay that vendor, for example. An "SoD conflict" is when one person's access lets them do both.
ICFR Internal Control over Financial Reporting
The full system of controls a company relies on to produce accurate financial statements. What SOX testing exists to verify, end to end.
Disclosure Controls
The controls that make sure a company tells the public what it's required to tell the public, on time — including the SEC's newer rules on disclosing cybersecurity incidents.
Population
Every transaction, user, or change that fits a specific test's criteria — the full set you'd sample from, not just the ones you happen to notice first.
Workpaper
The formal, structured record of a test: what you tested, what you found, and your conclusion. What you actually hand to a reviewer — and what this course grades you on.
Planning Card
TIB's own tool for slowing you down before you touch SAP: state the risk, the control objective, the population, and the evidence plan first. The habit the whole course is built around.
RCM Risk Control Matrix
The master spreadsheet mapping each business risk to the control meant to address it, and the test used to verify that control actually works.
Reasonable Assurance
The standard controls are actually held to — not perfect, not zero risk, but a level of confidence a reasonable person would find credible. Controls manage risk; they don't eliminate it.
Inside the system

SAP System Terms

The words that describe the system itself — what version, what screen, what path a change takes to go live.

S/4HANA
SAP's current-generation ERP, built on its in-memory HANA database. The direction almost every large SAP customer is migrating toward — and the live system you'll get access to in this course.
ECC ERP Central Component
SAP's previous-generation ERP. Still running inside many companies that are mid-migration to S/4HANA — you'll likely encounter both in your career.
Fiori
SAP's modern web and mobile interface, layered on top of S/4HANA. It introduces its own authorization objects that don't exist in the classic screens — a common blind spot in access testing.
T-code Transaction Code
The short code typed into SAP to open a specific screen or function — PFCG for role maintenance, for example. SAP's version of a keyboard shortcut.
Transport
The mechanism SAP uses to move a change from a development environment into production, through a controlled, approved path — rather than someone editing the live system directly.
DEV / QAS / PRD
The three-environment path most SAP changes travel: Development, Quality Assurance (testing), and Production (live). A change that skips a step here is exactly the kind of thing Book 2 is built around.
Basis
The technical infrastructure layer of SAP — and the team responsible for keeping the system running, patched, backed up, and available.
Firefighter Access
Temporary, logged, elevated access granted for emergencies — meant to be rare, time-boxed, and reviewed afterward. When it isn't reviewed, that's a deficiency.
Display-only Access
Read access with no ability to change anything. The standard, safe way to explore a live SAP system without risking a real change — the habit this course insists on from day one.
Who's involved

Regulatory & Professional Terms

The organizations and roles that make SOX testing a real job with real stakes, not just an internal exercise.

SEC Securities and Exchange Commission
The US regulator that enforces SOX and requires public companies to file accurate, timely financial disclosures.
PCAOB Public Company Accounting Oversight Board
The body that sets the standards external auditors must follow when they test a company's controls.
CEO/CFO Certification
The personal sign-off SOX requires from a company's CEO and CFO, certifying that the financial statements — and the controls behind them — are accurate. The reason a "minor" finding is never really minor to the person testing it.
Big 4
The four largest global audit and advisory firms — Deloitte, EY, KPMG, and PwC. The firms most SAP SOX/ITGC consultants eventually work for, or work alongside.
Internal Audit
The team inside a company that tests its own controls year-round, ahead of the external auditor's review — often the first place an SAP-fluent controls specialist gets hired.
External Audit
The independent audit firm hired to opine on whether a company's financial statements and controls can be trusted — usually one of the Big 4 or a large regional firm.
The platform itself

GRC Platform Terms

"GRC" also refers to a specific set of SAP modules — this is what each one actually does.

SAP GRC Access Control
The SAP module that manages who has access to what, screens for Segregation of Duties conflicts, and handles Firefighter access requests.
SAP GRC Process Control
The SAP module that manages the testing and documentation of business process controls — the kind you'll test in Modules 2 and 3 of this course.
IAG Identity Access Governance
SAP's cloud-based evolution of GRC Access Control, built for companies running a mix of on-premise and cloud systems.
Access Risk Analysis
The GRC function that scans a user's assigned access for Segregation of Duties conflicts, before or after that access is granted.
